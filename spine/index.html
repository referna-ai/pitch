Product strategy — a thought exercise

The spine of Referna is
the next client.
The product keeps forgetting it.

A before-and-after walk through every surface of the member journey — what each one says today, and what it would say if every screen, email, and meeting moved a member one step closer to a real client.

Internal — August 2026. Every "before" quote is taken verbatim from the live product code.
02
The frame

Every service has a spine — the one promise everything else must ladder up to.

"Figure out the spine of your service offering. Then check whether the product actually follows it — that's where most products fail."

— the CPO frame this exercise started from
Referna's spine
We help independent professionals land their next client by matching them into referral circles.

The golden goose is the next client. Circles, matches, meetings, points — all of it is scaffolding. A member who sits through every ritual and never gets closer to a client has been failed, however good the meetings felt.

04
The sharpest finding

The product manufactures its golden goose live on every call —
then drops it on the floor.

In every facilitated 1:1, Gordy pushes both members to name one real person they would refer to the other. The meeting summary even records whether a real lead was named. And then: no follow-up, no tracking, no "did the intro happen?", no surface where that lead ever appears again.

This is the single highest-leverage gap in the product. The moment a member says "talk to Dana at Nova — I'll introduce you tomorrow" is the moment Referna's promise comes true. Today that moment has no home. The data already exists; the loop is simply never closed.

The reframe
The unit of progress in Referna is not a meeting held.
It is a client opportunity in motion.
05
The alignment principle

Four rules for every surface that follows

1

Every screen answers: which client, and what moved?

If a page cannot say which client opportunity it advances, it is scaffolding — demote it. Faces, points, and charters are means, never the message.

2

The give comes before the get

Referral circles run on give-to-get. The mirror question — "which client need have you seen that someone else can serve?" — deserves equal billing everywhere the ask appears.

3

Never fake the goose

Where real client evidence is missing, say so honestly and go collect it. An invented "12 clients match your ICP" spends trust on the exact promise the spine depends on.

4

Every interaction leaves a lead, not a rating

The artifact of a meeting is a named opportunity with an owner and a next step — not "how did it go?". Track it to done, and the product's value becomes countable.

Before / after · Surface 1

Onboarding — strong on the client you want, silent on the clients you can give

Today

Ten steps that map you and your ideal client

"What does a great referral look like for you?"Step 4 — Ideal Referral Persona
"How many additional good-fit clients could you start serving in the next 3 months?"Step 5 — Referral Baseline

The take side is genuinely deep: ideal client, past client examples, trigger moments, capacity. But the give side — the fuel of every circle — is a single optional step ("What is a recent client need you could not help with directly?"), answerable with a shrug.

The member leaves as a well-described target — not as a contributor with a book to open.
Proposed

Two first-class declarations: the client you want, the clients you can open

  • "Name your next client." One concrete description of the client the member wants to land this quarter — the object the whole product then tracks.
  • "Open your book." Required, structured: three client needs seen in the last 90 days that were not for you — company type, the need, how you heard. This is the inventory circles trade on.
  • Success screen reframed from "We've identified 3 matches" to "3 people who can shorten the path to that client."
Every member arrives with a target to chase and gives to trade — matching gets richer data, circles get fuel from day one.
07
Before / after · Surface 2

The matches page — faces and roles up front, the client invented or buried

Today

A card about a person, not a route to a client

  • Lead content: photo, name, profession, timezone. The match reasoning hides in a collapsed "More context".
  • When real client data is missing, the card invents it: "12 of their clients match your ICP" — the number is derived from a hash of the person's name, and the "+N more" beside real client chips is fabricated too.
  • CTA: "I want to meet" / "Not now". Mutual selection celebrates with "It's a match" — dating-app language about the relationship, not the client.
The member is asked to want a meeting. The reason to want it — a path to a client — is either hidden, or fake.
Proposed

Every card leads with the real referral route — or an honest gap

  • Headline slot: the route, from real evidence only — "Serves seed-stage B2B SaaS · 3 named clients in your target market · sees your buying trigger monthly."
  • No evidence? Say so: "No client overlap mapped yet — one call maps it." Honesty here is the product working, not failing.
  • Kill the hash-count generator outright — it is trust debt on the exact promise we sell.
  • Match celebration: "A route to your next client just opened."
"I want to meet" becomes a decision about a client path, and every card is a claim we can stand behind.
08
Before / after · Surface 3

Emails — fifteen recurring templates, zero client-led subject lines

Today

Every subject is about peers, meetings, and the platform

  • "Your 3 new recommended peers on Referna are ready"
  • "Someone wants to connect with you on Referna"
  • "You have a new match on Referna"
  • "The Referna community is growing" / "New peers may be waiting for you"
  • "How did your meeting with Ben go?"

Across the entire recurring email set, no subject or opening line ever names a client, a lead, or a business outcome. The inbox — the surface members see most — sells membership upkeep, not the goose.

The channel with the highest repeat exposure carries the weakest version of the promise.
Proposed

The client rides in the subject line

  • "Dana can open doors to 3 companies in your target market" — match notification, real evidence only.
  • "Ben named a lead for you on yesterday's call — want the intro?" — post-meeting, the named lead is the headline.
  • "Your intro to Nova is 6 days old — nudge it?" — opportunity follow-through, not attendance follow-up.
  • Inactivity nudges reference the member's own declared next-client target: "Still hunting a seed-stage CFO seat? Two new routes appeared."
Every email answers "which client, and what moved" — or it doesn't send.
09
Before / after · Surface 4

The live meeting — the most spine-aligned surface we have. Keep it. Bank its output.

Today — mostly right

Gordy already runs the call at the client

"Follow my lead through a short structured session — it's what turns this into real value and future referrals, not just random networking."Gordy, call opener
"Name one real person you'd refer to the other."Gordy, Round 5

Backstory, a real client story, journey mapping, call signs ("the one clue that means: refer me"), and a named real referral. This is the spine, executed.

One flaw: the call's outputs — call signs, named leads — evaporate when the room closes.
Proposed

Same meeting — but everything said in the room becomes product state

  • Each named lead is captured as a structured object before the call ends: who, for whom, promised next step, by when — Gordy confirms it out loud.
  • Call signs ("refer me when you see ___") are saved to the member's profile and shown on their match card and roster entry from then on.
  • The closing clip changes from "Gordy will reach out for your honest read" to "Gordy will check on Thursday whether the Dana intro happened."
The room stops being a performance and becomes the pipeline's intake.
10
Before / after · Surface 5

After the meeting — today we ask about the relationship, never about the lead

Today

The follow-up is a membership question

"Would you like to add Ben to your circle? Adding someone means you're open to referring them — and being referred by them."Post-call decision nudge

The recap email and nudge both drive one decision: circle membership. The summary pipeline literally records whether a real lead was named on the call — a boolean sitting in the schema — and no email, page, or reminder ever mentions that lead again.

The one artifact that proves Referna works is collected and then abandoned.
Proposed

The follow-up is the lead's next step

  • Recap email leads with the exchange: "You promised Ben an intro to Dana at Nova. Ben promised you an intro to the Acme CFO."
  • One-tap actions: "Intro sent" / "Draft it for me" / "It fell through" — each updates the tracked opportunity.
  • Day-7 check: "The Dana intro hasn't moved. Want Gordy to nudge Ben?"
  • "Add to circle" still exists — one line, after the leads. Trust follows delivered referrals, not the other way around.
Every call now has a measurable afterlife: leads promised, intros made, clients landed.

Before / after · Surface 6

The cohort roster — an attendance sheet that should be a referral market

Today

Who showed up, and how often you've met them

"Everyone in your Founding Cohort track. Meet whoever looks interesting."Page header
  • Per member: photo, name, profession, a "Met 2x · last 12 days ago" pill, LinkedIn, "Schedule a 1:1".
  • Sections are attendance states: full members, pending, guests.
  • Client language exists only inside the profile modal, if the member wrote it.
"Meet whoever looks interesting" is the anti-spine: browsing people instead of chasing clients.
Proposed

An asks-and-gives board

  • Each member's card leads with their current ask — "hunting: seed-stage fintech needing fractional CFO" — pulled from their next-client declaration.
  • And their open gives: client needs they've spotted that they can't serve, visible to the room.
  • The roster sorts by relevance to your ask, not by attendance.
  • "Schedule a 1:1" becomes contextual: "Talk to Dana — her give matches your ask."
Ten seconds on the page answers "who in this room moves my next client forward — and whose client can I move?"
12
Before / after · Surface 7

The circle page — governance and points where the pipeline should be

Today

Charter, credits, cadence

  • Members, open seats, and a charter: meeting frequency, DM response time, voting rules, credit values.
  • Points badges on every member chip.
  • Business value appears only after the fact — the "Referral thank-you" flow with its "Estimated value of this business" slider is the one outcome-facing moment, and it's post-hoc.
The circle's home page describes how the club runs — not what the club is producing.
Proposed

The circle's shared pipeline is the page

  • Centerpiece: opportunities in motion — "4 intros in motion · 2 proposals out · 1 client landed this month," each with an owner and a next step.
  • Members ranked by referrals given, not points held — the give-first culture made visible.
  • The charter collapses to a settings drawer. Governance is plumbing, not the promise.
  • The thank-you flow stays — now it closes a tracked opportunity instead of appearing from nowhere.
A member opening the page sees the machine producing clients — or sees clearly that it isn't, which is the honest signal to fix the circle.

Measuring the spine

If the spine is the next client, the north star is
time to first referred client.

Everything else is an input metric on the way there — and every one of them is countable the moment leads become tracked objects:

L

Leads named per meeting

Already prompted on every call. The rate at which rooms produce real, named opportunities.

I

Intros completed within 7 days

The promise-keeping rate — the give-to-get engine's pulse, and the thing Gordy should chase.

C

Leads converted to clients

The goose itself. Reported by members through the thank-you flow that already exists.

T

The member-visible tracker

A persistent "Your next client" module on every page: "2 intros in motion · 1 proposal out." The spine, always on screen.

Where to start

Five slices, each shippable and testable on its own

Stop faking the goose

Remove the hash-generated client counts from match cards; show real evidence or an honest gap. Smallest change, immediate trust repair.

Days

Close the named-lead loop

Lead named on a call becomes a tracked object with a recap email, one-tap status, and a day-7 nudge. The data already exists — this is the highest-leverage slice in the deck.

1–2 weeks

Put the client in the subject line

Rewrite the recurring email set so every subject names a client, a lead, or a movement. Copy-level change, touches the highest-frequency surface.

Days

Asks and gives on the roster

Add the next-client declaration and open-your-book step to onboarding; surface both on cohort and match cards. Turns rosters into markets.

2–3 weeks

The circle pipeline page

Rebuild the circle page around opportunities in motion, with the "Your next client" tracker module rolled out product-wide.

3–4 weeks
Each slice ends with something a member can click and feel — no phase ships without a user-visible change.
